HARBOUR LOG
Kept at the east wall, one entry a day.

Day 1
  Clear and cold, frost on the rail.
  The harbour master counted the buoys and found the red one adrift.

Day 2
  Squalls after noon.
  A seal slept on the slip all afternoon and would not be moved.

Day 3
  Wind from the north-east, steady.
  Two crab boats tied up at the east wall and paid their dues into the ledger.
  Entered three arrivals and one departure in the ledger.

Day 4
  Low cloud, sea like slate.
  Children fished for mackerel from the steps until the tide turned.

Day 5
  Still air, the water flat as a table.
  Oiled the winch and replaced the rope on the harbour crane.

Day 6
  Rain most of the morning.
  Trimmed the lantern wick and cleaned the glass before dusk.

Day 7
  A warm southerly, gulls restless.
  Checked the tide table against the mark on the wall; it ran four inches high.

Day 8
  Fog until nine, then a thin sun.
  The night tide brought weed over the lower step.

Day 9
  Clear and cold, frost on the rail.
  A schooner from the south took on water and salt, then left on the evening tide.
  Entered three arrivals and one departure in the ledger.

Day 10
  Squalls after noon.
  The lantern was lit early because of the fog.

Day 11
  Wind from the north-east, steady.
  A stranger asked the way to the lantern room and was shown up.

Day 12
  Low cloud, sea like slate.
  Mended the mooring chain by the slip; one link was worn half through.
  Trimmed the lantern wick and cleaned the glass before dusk.
